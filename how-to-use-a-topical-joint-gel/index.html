How to Use a Topical Joint Gel Safely

By the editorial team. Updated 9 October 2026.

See a GP or physio. Persistent, worsening or unexplained joint pain should be assessed by a GP or physiotherapist. This page is general information, not medical advice.

Gels and creams for sore joints are easy to misuse: too much, on irritated skin, or in place of getting a problem checked. This general guide covers sensible use of a topical gel or massage gel on the knees, hands and shoulders. Always follow the label on the product you actually have. For Kinzeno, the seller says to use a coin-sized amount and massage it in, and states it can be used two to four times a day.

Step 1: check the product and your skin

  • Read the ingredient list and directions on the pack.
  • Do not apply to broken, sunburnt or inflamed skin.
  • Do a patch test on the inner forearm the day before, and wait 24 hours.

The patch test matters most with botanical products, because plant extracts such as arnica are among the more common triggers of contact skin reactions. Our guide to side effects and who should avoid it lists who should ask a pharmacist first.

Step 2: apply a small amount

Start with less than you think you need, around the size of a five-cent piece for a knee, as most gels spread easily. According to the seller, a coin-sized amount is enough for Kinzeno. More gel does not mean more effect, and it makes irritation more likely.

Step 3: massage gently

Use light circular movements around the joint for a minute or two, working along the muscles above and below it. Massage should feel comfortable. If it hurts, stop. Avoid pressing hard on a joint that is hot, swollen or injured.

Many people find that gentle movement after a massage, such as a few knee bends or a slow walk, feels better than sitting still. This is where an exercise plan from a physiotherapist adds the most value.

Step 4: wash hands and keep it away from eyes

Wash your hands after applying, unless the hands are the area being treated, in which case avoid touching your eyes and mouth until the gel has dried. Keep the container closed, away from heat and out of reach of children.

What to avoid

  • Do not use heat packs, hot water bottles or tight bandages over freshly applied gel unless advised by a health professional.
  • Do not combine several different gels on the same area without asking a pharmacist.
  • Do not exceed the directions on the label.
  • Do not use a gel to push through a sharp or worsening pain.

When to stop and see someone

Stop if you see a rash, blistering, swelling or itching. See your GP if pain lasts for more than a few days, wakes you at night, follows an injury, or if the joint is hot, red or swollen. Healthdirect advises seeing a GP for joint pain, sooner if you have trouble walking, reduced movement or grinding or clicking. Call 000 in an emergency. Storing the gel and tracking whether it helps

Store gels in a cool place, with the cap closed, and discard them if the smell, colour or texture changes. Check the expiry or period-after-opening symbol on the pack.

If you try a gel, keep a simple diary for two weeks. Each day, note your pain from zero to ten at the same time, what you did, and whether you used the gel. This tells you far more than a vague impression, and it is useful to show a GP or physio. Because pain naturally varies, change only one thing at a time: if you start a new exercise, a new medicine and a new gel in the same week, you will not know which made a difference.

If the diary shows no improvement after a couple of weeks, stop buying the product and put your effort into the things with better evidence. It is entirely reasonable to decide that a massage gel is a pleasant comfort item and nothing more.

Frequently asked questions

How much gel should I use on a knee?

Use the amount in the directions on the label. As a rule, start small. The seller says a coin-sized amount is enough for Kinzeno.

How often can I apply a massage gel?

Follow the label. The seller states Kinzeno can be used two to four times a day, but ask a pharmacist if you are unsure, or if you use other products.

Can I use a gel before exercise?

Many people apply gel after a warm-up or after exercise. Avoid heat on top of freshly applied gel unless a health professional suggests it.

Does a gel replace seeing a physio?

No. A gel can make a massage more pleasant, but a physiotherapist can assess the cause and prescribe exercise, which has the strongest evidence for joint pain.
